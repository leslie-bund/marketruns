# Nigerian marketplace compliance, participant onboarding, theft response, and R2 media retention

**Research date / primary-source access date: 2026-11-14.** Scope: Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 (FCCPA), Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA), NDPC General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 (GAID), Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 (CAMA), NIMC identity-access materials, criminal-justice statutes, Cloudflare R2 documentation, and Market Runs' existing doctrine. No application code, migrations, or configuration were changed.

**Implementation update:** ADR-0031 implements the finite product/intent retention schedule, private capture prefixes, durable deletion records, scheduled cleanup, and admin holds. Buyer intent media remains fresh camera capture; no WebView file-upload or RASP trust decision was adopted. Standard R2 storage remains the default.

**Legal-review boundary.** This is issue-spotting and product-policy research, not a legal opinion. **Qualified Nigerian counsel should validate the final policy, participant contracts, refund rules, employment/gig classification, State-specific criminal procedure, and retention schedule before launch.** State law and sector-specific product rules may add requirements. The labels below distinguish **verified requirements** from **recommendations / optional risk controls**; a recommendation is not presented as a statutory mandate.

## Executive findings

- The FCCPA applies to commercial activities in Nigeria and covers both goods and services. Market Runs should assume that its own marketplace/errand services, sellers, and runners can all be relevant “undertakings”; calling a runner internal or gig does not switch off consumer rights. The precise allocation of liability between platform, seller, and runner remains fact- and contract-specific. [FCCPA, ss. 2 and 167](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
- The current Act already requires plain-language notices, price display, honest descriptions, transaction records, fair dealing, service quality, and meaningful return/refund remedies. A 30-minute product dispute window, a “no refunds” clause, or an internal settlement cap cannot be used to extinguish statutory rights. [FCCPA, ss. 114–132 and 127–129](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
- FCCPC's omnibus **Consumer Protection Regulations 2026 are still published as exposure drafts** on the official regulations page. Their online-marketplace identity, distance-contract, complaint, and five-year record proposals are useful design targets but are **not verified here as final binding law**. Check the Federal Government Official Gazette and FCCPC again before relying on them. [FCCPC regulations page](https://fccpc.gov.ng/resources-library/regulations/), [FCCPC exposure notice](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/FCCPC-Consumer-Protection-Exposure-Notice.pdf)
- Onboarding must be purpose-limited and proportionate. Verify the person and payout destination, but do not retain raw NIN/BVN, NIN slips, full identity documents, guarantor files, or criminal-record material by default. NIMC's current NINAuth flow supports consent-driven verification and a share code that avoids exposing the full NIN. [NDPA, ss. 24–30 and 65](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf), [NIMC FAQs 2026, section 7](https://nimc.gov.ng/storage/Uploads/NIMC_FAQs_2026.docx)
- Runner theft requires two separate tracks: prompt consumer redress under marketplace/consumer rules and, where evidence supports it, a factual police report. Platform suspension and settlement are not criminal findings. Violence, public shaming, coerced repayment, unauthorised searches, and routine private detention are not acceptable controls. [FCCPA, ss. 124 and 130](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf), [Administration of Criminal Justice Act 2015 (ACJA), ss. 2, 5–8, 20 and 23](https://justice.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Administration-of-Justice-Criminal-Act-2015.pdf)
- Replace immediate product-media deletion with a **bounded, purpose-specific grace period**: seven days for expired/unaccepted listings and 90 days after delivery for accepted goods, followed by deletion unless a narrow incident/legal hold applies. Keep run/handoff/dispute evidence on its existing separate path. R2 lifecycle rules automate expiry; bucket locks can override lifecycle deletion, but neither should become an excuse for indefinite retention. [Cloudflare R2 object lifecycles](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/), [Cloudflare R2 bucket locks](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/bucket-locks/)

## 1. Consumer-law obligations

### 1.1 Scope and responsibility

**Verified requirements.** FCCPA section 2 covers all undertakings and commercial activities within or having effect in Nigeria. Section 167 defines a consumer to include a purchaser of goods other than for resale and a person to whom a service is rendered; “service” includes services of any description and goods sold with a service; an undertaking includes a person producing or trading in goods or providing services. Market Runs therefore cannot safely treat its digital venue, internal delivery, or future gig delivery as outside the Act. [FCCPA, ss. 2 and 167](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)

The platform should disclose who supplies the goods, who supplies delivery, who collects the buyer's money, and who handles each remedy. That allocation must match actual conduct: section 136 makes the undertaking supplying defective goods or services liable for resulting damage whether or not the consumer bought directly from it, while sections 142–144 imply reasonable care, skill, and timely performance into business services and prevent consumer-facing exclusion of those duties. [FCCPA, ss. 136 and 142–144](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)

**Recommendation.** Use separate, plain-language buyer, seller, internal-runner, and gig-runner terms, but present one coherent checkout and redress experience. Do not tell a buyer to pursue a disappeared seller or runner as the only remedy where Market Runs supplied part of the service, controlled acceptance, held the funds, or made the relevant representation. This is a risk-control and contract-design recommendation; the exact legal allocation needs counsel's review.

### 1.2 E-commerce disclosures and pricing

**Verified requirements under the current Act:**

- A notice, document, or visual representation required for a consumer must use the prescribed form or, absent one, plain language understandable to the intended ordinary consumer. Material liability limits, consumer assumptions of risk, indemnities, and acknowledgements must be conspicuous and comprehensible before the consumer commits. [FCCPA, ss. 114 and 128](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
- Goods or services displayed for sale must have an adequately displayed price in Nigerian currency. The consumer cannot be required to pay above the displayed price or, where multiple prices are concurrently displayed, above the lowest. A product description must not mislead; used, second-hand, reconditioned, rebuilt, or remade goods need a conspicuous status notice. [FCCPA, ss. 115–117](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
- Marketing and offers must not contain false, erroneous, fraudulent, deceptive, or materially misleading representations about nature, properties, uses, supply conditions, price, quality, or warranties. Section 125 also provides damages and monetary restitution for misleading material representations and requires correction of an apparent misapprehension. Product photos, videos, seller claims, runner claims, urgency cues, and delivery promises all need to be accurate. [FCCPA, ss. 123–126](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
- An advance order may be cancelled subject to a reasonable cancellation charge, assessed using factors such as notice, the goods/services, the chance of finding another customer, and industry practice. No cancellation fee is permitted where death or hospitalisation prevents the consumer from honouring the booking. [FCCPA, s. 120](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)

**Recommended checkout disclosure.** Before the buyer accepts, show the seller's legal or registered business name and contact route, Market Runs' identity and contact route, the actual goods and quantity/condition, the accepted goods price, delivery charge, every mandatory fee and tax, total NGN amount, expected pickup/delivery arrangements, substitution/cancellation rules, refund route, complaint channel, and any material liability term. Require affirmative acceptance of the final offer; a changed item or price is a new offer, not silent consent. Some of these details are express proposals in the 2026 exposure draft rather than verified current online-specific duties; the current Act's plain-language, price, misleading-representation, and conspicuous-term rules independently support them. [FCCPA, ss. 114–115, 123 and 128](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf), [2026 exposure draft, regs. 4–5 and 13](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/FCCP-Consumer-Protection-Regulations-Exposure-Draft-.pdf)

### 1.3 Returns, refunds, service failure, and liability

**Verified requirements:**

- In addition to other unsafe/defective-goods remedies, section 122 gives a full refund where goods are found unsuitable for a communicated particular purpose within a reasonable time, or where unexamined goods are rejected within a reasonable time because they do not correspond with the description/sample or contemplated type and quality. [FCCPA, s. 122](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
- Consumers are entitled to suitable, good-quality, defect-free, reasonably durable goods and applicable regulatory standards. Within **three months after delivery**, goods failing those standards may be returned without penalty at the supplying undertaking's risk and expense; the undertaking must repair, replace, or refund the price. The undertaking alleging that goods or services were not defective bears the statutory onus under section 145. [FCCPA, ss. 131–132 and 145](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
- For services, the consumer has rights to timely performance, notice of unavoidable delay, reasonably expected quality, and return of consumer property in at least as good condition. For deficient performance, the consumer may require remediation or a reasonable portion of the price back. This directly matters when a runner loses, damages, substitutes, or fails to deliver entrusted goods. [FCCPA, s. 130](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
- Unfair, unreasonable, unjust, excessively one-sided, hidden, or deceptive terms are prohibited. Terms cannot waive statutory return rights, avoid FCCPA duties, authorise unlawful conduct, or exclude loss attributable to gross negligence. The Act also prohibits requiring a consumer to deposit an identity document, bank card, account-access card, or account-access code as a contract term. [FCCPA, ss. 127–129](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
- Liability for damage caused wholly or partly by defective goods or service supply cannot be excluded; specified implied obligations concerning title, description, quality, fitness, and services also cannot be excluded against consumers. [FCCPA, ss. 136–144](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)

**Policy consequence for this repository.** [ADR-0007's evidence-first settlement](../adr/0007-dispute-resolution-policy.md), [ADR-0008's “aborts never refund fully” rule](../adr/0008-aborts-never-refund-fully.md), and the 30-minute dispute window are internal allocation mechanisms, not a lawful ceiling on consumer remedies. Where the FCCPA requires a full refund or other redress, the settlement function must be able to express that lawful outcome. Perishable goods, immediate errands, abandoned orders, and the interaction of partial aborts with sections 120, 122, 130, and 132 need explicit Nigerian counsel review rather than a blanket “no full refund” rule.

### 1.4 Sales, accounting, and complaint records

**Verified requirements.** Except where impracticable, the consumer must receive a written record of each sale/supply containing at least the undertaking's full or registered business name, supply premises address, date, description, unit prices, quantities, pre-tax total, applicable taxes, and total including taxes. Electronic order history/receipts can serve this purpose. Section 118 does **not itself state a retention duration**. [FCCPA, s. 118](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)

For Allegados Limited as a company, CAMA separately requires accounting records that explain transactions, receipts/expenditure, assets/liabilities, and relevant goods dealings; those accounting records must generally be preserved for **six years from creation**, with electronic copies permitted subject to appropriate technology and security. That is an accounting-record rule, not a requirement to retain every product photo for six years. [CAMA, ss. 374–375](https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/assets/docs/CAMA-NOTE%20FINAL-FULL-VERSION.pdf)

**Recommendation.** Retain the structured receipt, accepted offer, seller/runner identity references, payment/settlement/refund state, delivery events, complaint, and resolution under a documented schedule. Keep media only where it serves a separate, defined purpose. Do not import the exposure draft's proposed five-year transaction-record retention into policy as though it were already law; monitor its final status. [2026 exposure draft, reg. 8](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/FCCP-Consumer-Protection-Regulations-Exposure-Draft-.pdf)

### 1.5 Complaints, redress, and escalation

**Verified requirements.** A consumer may refer a dispute directly to the supplying undertaking, the applicable sector regulator, or FCCPC, and may directly approach a competent court. FCCPC may investigate, issue orders/compliance notices, make consent orders including damages, and consumers retain civil compensation/restitution rights. General contravention of consumer rights can also constitute an offence under section 155. [FCCPA, ss. 146–155](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)

FCCPC's official process supports portal, app, email, letter, and walk-in complaints, with attachments such as receipts, agreements, and correspondence. Its FAQ asks consumers to engage the provider first and says redress commonly takes 1–45 days, but exceptions exist; this is FCCPC process guidance, not permission for Market Runs to impose a 30-minute exclusive limitation period. [FCCPC complaint handling](https://fccpc.gov.ng/consumers/complaint-handling/), [FCCPC FAQs](https://fccpc.gov.ng/about-us/faqs/)

**Recommended controls.** Provide a free in-app complaint route plus a published support contact, automatic acknowledgement and tracking ID, access to the receipt and evidence, a named owner, a reasoned outcome, and an appeal/review path. Publish FCCPC escalation and court rights without forcing arbitration or an internal process as a jurisdictional bar. Keep safety/product-hazard complaints separate from ordinary negotiation expiry; FCCPA sections 133–135 support monitoring, notification, recall, and withdrawal of hazardous goods. [FCCPA, ss. 133–135 and 146–150](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)

### 1.6 Proposed 2026 rules: design now, do not misstate status

The official exposure draft would expressly add affirmative digital consent, all-in pricing, five-year transaction-record retention, detailed defective-goods procedures, distance-contract identity/contact/delivery/redress disclosures, risk passing on actual delivery, marketplace seller identity/contact verification, complaint escalation, reasonable help for non-delivery/fraud/disappearing sellers, and notice-and-action for unlawful listings. These are sensible launch controls, but the draft says commencement depends on Official Gazette publication and the official page still labels it an exposure draft. [2026 exposure draft, regs. 4–5, 8, 11, 13 and 18](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/FCCP-Consumer-Protection-Regulations-Exposure-Draft-.pdf), [FCCPC status page](https://fccpc.gov.ng/resources-library/regulations/)

## 2. Lawful seller and runner onboarding

### 2.1 Verified NDPA/NDPC duties

- Personal data must be fair, lawful, transparent, collected for specified explicit legitimate purposes, adequate and relevant but **limited to the minimum necessary**, retained no longer than necessary, accurate, and secured. The controller owes a duty of care and must demonstrate accountability. A lawful basis must be identified for each processing purpose; contract, legal obligation, vital/public interest, legitimate interests, or consent may apply depending on the facts. [NDPA, ss. 24–25](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)
- Consent must be affirmative, informed, specific, provable, and withdrawable; silence is not consent. Making service conditional on unnecessary processing undermines free consent. Before collection, the notice must identify the controller, purposes and lawful basis, recipients, rights, retention period, NDPC complaint right, and relevant automated decision-making. [NDPA, ss. 26–27](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)
- Biometric data used to uniquely identify a person is sensitive personal data. Section 30 requires a specific permitted condition; GAID article 18 calls for consent for sensitive-data processing. The current face-enrollment and ArcFace design therefore needs specific, prominent consent and the existing DPIA/security controls, not merely a general marketplace acceptance. [NDPA, ss. 30 and 65](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf), [GAID, arts. 17–18](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf)
- A DPIA is required for likely high-risk processing. GAID article 28 expressly lists e-commerce, sensitive/highly personal data, systematic monitoring, profiling, and significant automated decisions, requires filing with NDPC in those cases, vetting by a certified DPO, and completion before processing. Runner location and face systems should be included, not assessed as an isolated signup form. [NDPA, s. 28](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf), [GAID, art. 28](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf)
- Processors need appropriate safeguards and written processing terms. Cross-border transfers need a lawful basis and documented adequacy/safeguards. Security must be proportionate to sensitivity, harm, processing extent, retention, and available controls. A risky breach requires NDPC notice within 72 hours; a high-risk breach requires immediate data-subject communication. [NDPA, ss. 29 and 39–43](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)
- NDPC's registration guidance expressly includes e-commerce and contains data-volume/sensitivity classifications. A data controller of major importance must register and designate a DPO; GAID also addresses compliance audits, returns, and internal reporting. Market Runs should complete the classification/registration workflow rather than assume an early-stage exemption. [NDPA, ss. 32 and 44](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf), [NDPC registration guidance](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Updated-Guidance-Notice-on-Registtration-2024.pdf), [GAID, arts. 7–14](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf)
- Individuals have access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection, and consent-withdrawal rights subject to lawful exceptions. A solely automated decision with legal or similarly significant effect is restricted and requires safeguards including human intervention, an opportunity to state the person's position, and contestation. [NDPA, ss. 34–37](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)

**No blanket KYC conclusion.** Neither the NDPA nor CAMA creates a general rule that every marketplace seller or runner must give Market Runs a raw BVN/NIN or an identity-document copy. Those identifiers may support a legitimate identity/fraud purpose, but the platform must still prove necessity, proportionality, notice, lawful basis, retention, and vendor authority. Payment-provider or regulated-product duties should be identified separately rather than inflated into universal collection. [ADR-0004's universal KYC decision](../adr/0004-universal-kyc.md) remains a deliberate product/security choice, not proof of a statutory mandate.

### 2.2 NIMC-specific verification route

NIMC's Access to Registered Information Regulations allow consent-based identity verification by licensed private agencies, restrict the information and purpose, require secure/audited access, and prohibit unauthorised use or disclosure. NIMC's 2026 FAQ describes NINAuth as the official NIN authentication channel for government and private services: access is consent-driven, organisations can use the official API, and an individual can supply a time-bound share code without exposing the full NIN. The FAQ also says adoption is mandated for MDAs and relevant service providers for NIN-based verification; counsel and the identity vendor should confirm the current licensing/integration route for Market Runs rather than assuming that any NIN lookup is authorised. [NIMC Access Regulations 2017, regs. 4, 7 and 9](https://nimc.gov.ng/pdfs/policies/NIMCaccess_register.pdf), [NIMC FAQs 2026, section 7](https://nimc.gov.ng/storage/Uploads/NIMC_FAQs_2026.docx), [NINAuth privacy policy](https://ninauth.nimc.gov.ng/legal/privacy-policy)

**Recommended implementation:** prefer NINAuth share-code/consent verification where supported; record the verification result, provider reference, consent/receipt, and minimum returned fields. Do not retain the full NIN slip or raw NIN by default. A keyed hash is still pseudonymous personal data, not anonymous data, so keep it protected, access-restricted, and limited to duplicate/fraud prevention. [NDPA, ss. 24, 39 and 65](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)

### 2.3 Proportionate approval checklist

The following are **recommended controls**, not a list of statutory documents every participant must supply:

**Every seller**

1. Verify the adult individual or authorised business representative, legal name, service contact, store/display name, pickup location, and payout-account ownership/name match. Keep public interactions on a display name and restrict legal/payout identity to authorised workflows.
2. Obtain an accuracy, lawful-inventory, product-safety, pricing, refund-cooperation, and prohibited-goods commitment. Apply category-specific licences/registration only when the actual goods require them (for example food, drugs, cosmetics, medical devices, chemicals, or packaged water); FCCPC identifies NAFDAC and SON as relevant sector regulators. [FCCPC FAQs](https://fccpc.gov.ng/about-us/faqs/)
3. Use risk-based step-up checks for unusual payout changes, repeated fraud, high-value goods, or inconsistent business details. Do not collect passport photographs, next-of-kin documents, full bank statements, or tax documents merely “because KYC”.

**CAC status where applicable**

- CAMA section 814 does **not** require every individual seller to incorporate. An individual trading under the true surname with only true forenames/initials falls outside business-name registration; a firm, individual, or company using a name beyond the statutory true-name formulation must register that business name. Registration is therefore conditional on legal form and trading name. [CAMA, ss. 814–815](https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/assets/docs/CAMA-NOTE%20FINAL-FULL-VERSION.pdf)
- For a trade name, request the business-name registration number and verify it directly in CAC's public search. For a company, verify the RC number/status and representative's authority; use the beneficial-ownership register only for a defined compliance purpose, not as a general data scrape. A certificate upload is a fallback when registry verification is unavailable or inconsistent, not the default archive. [CAC company search](https://www.cac.gov.ng/services/company-search), [CAC public search](https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/public-search/), [CAC beneficial-ownership register](https://bor.cac.gov.ng/)
- A registered business using a business name must include prescribed true-name and registration details on receipts, invoices, advertisements, and other official business documents. Build receipt data from verified registry fields rather than storing certificate images indefinitely. [CAMA, s. 820](https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/assets/docs/CAMA-NOTE%20FINAL-FULL-VERSION.pdf)

**Every runner (internal or gig)**

1. Verify adult identity, contact, payout destination, service area, device/session security, and acceptance of chain-of-custody, safety, privacy, evidence, suspension, and incident-cooperation rules. Use the same core identity decision for internal and gig runners, then apply the appropriate employment/contractor documents separately.
2. Request a driver's licence, vehicle registration, insurance, or roadworthiness evidence **only for the transport mode and jurisdiction that actually requires it**. FRSC's official licensing service states that a driver's-licence applicant must be at least 18 and complete the applicable process. Do not collect vehicle documents from a walking/bicycle runner without another demonstrated purpose. [FRSC driver licensing](https://nigeriadriverslicence.frsc.gov.ng/)
3. Treat guarantors, criminal-record checks, emergency contacts, continuous background location, and repeated face checks as separate high-risk processing decisions. Require a written necessity/proportionality assessment, appropriate notice/lawful basis, restricted access, and a short retention/review schedule; do not make them default onboarding bundles. GAID's legitimate-interest template expressly asks whether less data or a less intrusive method can achieve the purpose and calls for particular caution with criminal-offence data. [GAID, art. 26 and Schedule 8](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf)
4. Give rejected or suspended applicants a meaningful correction and human-review route where the decision significantly affects them; do not treat a vendor score, face mismatch, route anomaly, or automated theft flag as conclusive guilt. [NDPA, s. 37](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)

## 3. Runner theft: lawful prevention and response

### 3.1 Separate four decisions

1. **Consumer restoration:** determine non-delivery, damage, substitution, or service failure and provide the required remedy without waiting for a criminal conviction. FCCPA service-quality, property-return, refund, liability, and redress provisions apply independently of a criminal prosecution. [FCCPA, ss. 130, 136 and 146–152](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)
2. **Platform action:** pause new assignments, preserve evidence, assess contract breaches, decide suspension/reinstatement, and apply the disclosed settlement rules with human review. These are administrative/contract decisions, not declarations that a person committed a crime.
3. **Criminal enforcement:** where facts support dishonest taking or conversion, make a factual report to the Nigeria Police Force or other competent authority. The Criminal Code defines stealing to include fraudulent taking **or conversion**, including property already in the converter's possession; the general offence is in section 390. The Act itself recognises State-law operation and the northern Penal Code, so counsel must select the applicable State offence and procedure rather than applying one nationwide playbook. [Criminal Code Act, ss. 1A, 2, 383 and 390 — statute text hosted by UNODC](https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/nga/1916/criminal_code_act_html/Nigeria_Criminal_Code_Act_1916.pdf)
4. **Privacy/data response:** preserve and disclose only relevant records under a documented lawful basis. Keep a disclosure log, verify the recipient and request scope, and do not publish a runner's legal identity, face, NIN/BVN, home address, or allegation to unrelated participants. NDPA principles and security duties continue during an investigation. [NDPA, ss. 24–25, 27 and 39–40](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)

### 3.2 Recommended product controls

- Bind one approved runner account/device to each assignment; prohibit account sharing; require step-up authentication for payout changes and suspicious recovery. Keep ordinary sessions/device credentials separate from sensitive biometric step-up.
- Capture the accepted item/quantity/price, seller pickup confirmation, one-time QR challenge, handoff photograph, timestamps, and buyer delivery confirmation. The repository already requires a photograph for confirmation, prevents photoless settlement, merges dispute evidence server-side, and carries it into retirement archives. [ADR-0020](../adr/0020-handoffs-require-photo-evidence.md), [ADR-0019](../adr/0019-handoff-evidence-on-the-wire.md), [ADR-0026](../adr/0026-preflight-fixes.md)
- Use tamper-evident packaging/seal numbers where proportionate, an exception reason for missing/damaged items, and buyer/seller correction channels. A photograph proves only what it depicts; it does not by itself prove identity, all package contents, or criminal intent. ADR-0019 likewise records that current photo references are not tamper-free provenance.
- Apply transparent run-value/quantity limits and step-up review for unusual risk. Pause **new** assignments on a credible incident while allowing the runner to respond; do not automatically confiscate unrelated payouts or label an unresolved anomaly “theft”.
- Use foreground/active-run location only for coordination and investigation as disclosed. Route deviation, GPS loss, camera failure, or connectivity failure is a risk signal, not proof of theft. [The privacy notice already limits runner GPS to foreground activity](../privacy-notice.md), and NDPA minimisation applies. [NDPA, ss. 24–25](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)
- Maintain an auditable incident packet: run/receipt IDs, chain-of-custody events, original media references and hashes, access history, runner/buyer/seller statements, settlement action, police reference, and evidence-release/deletion dates. Keep it role-restricted and time-limited.

### 3.3 Response sequence and self-help limits

**Recommended response:** secure people first; stop further assignments; contact affected participants; preserve only relevant evidence; acknowledge and assess the consumer claim; give the runner a chance to explain; make a reasoned platform decision; resolve money through the settlement function; and report credible suspected theft to police with the incident packet. A victim report need not wait for a court order, but broader compelled disclosure should be handled under the applicable legal process with counsel.

For the FCT and federal offences, ACJA section 20 gives a private person a limited arrest power where an offence is committed in that person's presence or where the person reasonably suspects an offence for which police may arrest without warrant; section 23 requires immediate handover to police. The ACJA also prohibits unnecessary restraint, requires notification of reasons/rights and humane treatment, prohibits torture, and says a suspect must not be arrested merely for a civil wrong or breach of contract. Its territorial scope is not nationwide State procedure, so equivalent State law must be checked. [ACJA, ss. 2, 5–8, 14, 20 and 23](https://justice.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Administration-of-Justice-Criminal-Act-2015.pdf)

**Do not operationalise private arrest as a routine theft-control feature.** Default to police assistance. Do not use force, threats, harassment, public naming/shaming, contact-list pressure, home entry, unauthorised phone/account searches, seizure of unrelated property, detention pending payment, or threats to expose debt/allegations to third parties. FCCPA section 124 independently prohibits physical force, coercion, undue pressure, harassment, and unfair tactics in supply, enforcement, and payment collection. [FCCPA, s. 124](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf)

## 4. Safer, cost-bounded product-media lifecycle

### 4.1 Existing doctrine and legal constraint

ADR-0010 deliberately deletes product photos and videos at product retirement and omits them from durable product/cart snapshots, while handoff/dispute evidence follows a separate retention case. ADR-0019/0020 make handoff photos evidence and require them for confirmation; ADR-0026 persists the dispute record at run retirement. [ADR-0010](../adr/0010-state-mapping-do-postgres-r2.md), [ADR-0019](../adr/0019-handoff-evidence-on-the-wire.md), [ADR-0020](../adr/0020-handoffs-require-photo-evidence.md), [ADR-0026](../adr/0026-preflight-fixes.md)

No reviewed FCCPA provision requires immediate deletion at retirement. Conversely, NDPA section 24 requires storage no longer than necessary and section 34 requires erasure where data is no longer necessary or no lawful retention basis remains. A longer media life therefore needs a named purpose—accurate-description complaints, non-delivery/theft investigation, legal claims—not indefinite “just in case” storage. [NDPA, ss. 24 and 34](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)

### 4.2 Recommended lifecycle

| Media class | Recommended default | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active listing | Keep only while the product is live; remove public access immediately on retirement | Retirement remains final; retention never resurrects the ProductDO |
| Expired, rejected, or unaccepted product | Private seven-day grace after retirement, then delete | Handles accidental/support races without keeping every listing |
| Accepted product tied to a run | Keep privately until **90 days after final delivery**, then delete unless held | Creates a bounded record relevant to the FCCPA's three-month defective-goods return right; this is a policy choice, not a statutory media period [FCCPA, s. 132](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FCCPA-2018.pdf) |
| Handoff/dispute evidence | Keep on the separate evidence schedule; never inherit ordinary product deletion | Existing ADR doctrine and evidence-first dispute handling |
| Theft, regulator, litigation, or formal legal hold | Copy/reference only incident-relevant objects into a restricted evidence prefix/bucket; record trigger, owner, lawful basis, start, review date, and release; delete after the justified tail | Prevents broad permanent media accumulation and supports legal-claims needs [NDPA, ss. 24, 30(1)(e) and 34](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf) |
| Abandoned/incomplete upload | Abort and clean up promptly | Avoids paying for unusable multipart fragments [Cloudflare upload documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/objects/upload-objects/) |

At minimum, persist the structured transaction record and media metadata (capture reference, hash, timestamps, associated product/run, access history) without treating the image itself as permanently necessary. Give the buyer a durable receipt even when the image expires. Update the privacy notice and DPIA with the actual periods, hold criteria, access roles, cross-border basis, and deletion process before adopting this schedule. [NDPA, ss. 27–28](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)

### 4.3 Verified Cloudflare mechanics

- **Lifecycle expiry:** R2 lifecycle rules support prefix filters, deletion after a number of days or on a date, transition to Infrequent Access, and aborting incomplete multipart uploads. Objects are typically removed within 24 hours of the applicable expiration, but changes for existing objects and large buckets can take longer. Deletion is therefore asynchronous, not a same-request guarantee; an object stops being billable once deleted. [Cloudflare object lifecycles](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/)
- **Bucket locks:** prefix-scoped bucket-lock rules can protect existing/new objects from overwrite and deletion for a duration, until a date, or indefinitely. The longest applicable rule wins. A lock overrides lifecycle expiry—for example, a 90-day lock prevents a 30-day lifecycle deletion—and blocked S3 deletes return an object-locked error. Cloudflare documents administrative removal of lock rules, so locks are an operational safeguard, not proof of an unalterable evidence trail; audit configuration changes. [Cloudflare bucket locks](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/bucket-locks/)
- **No S3 legal-hold API assumption:** R2's current compatibility table marks `x-amz-object-lock-mode`, `x-amz-object-lock-retain-until-date`, and `x-amz-object-lock-legal-hold` unimplemented, along with governance-bypass headers. Use the documented bucket-lock feature and application hold state, not AWS object-lock code assumed to work unchanged. [Cloudflare S3 API compatibility](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/s3/api/)
- **Irrecoverability:** emptying/deleting objects cannot be undone. Use restricted private buckets, least-privilege API tokens, dual review for manual purge, and deletion reconciliation rather than treating lifecycle configuration as a backup or evidence-preservation system. [Cloudflare delete-buckets/object-deletion documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/delete-buckets/)

**Recommended control plane:** separate `products/live/`, `products/grace/`, and `evidence/<run-or-incident>/` policies (or separate buckets); write a durable deletion/hold marker before relying on asynchronous lifecycle deletion; alert on expired objects still present; test that holds block both application and lifecycle deletion; and verify that ordinary product deletion never touches handoff/dispute evidence. Avoid indefinite bucket locks for ordinary products because they frustrate minimisation, erasure, and cost control.

### 4.4 Cost choice

At the access date, Cloudflare lists Standard storage at **$0.015/GB-month** and Infrequent Access at **$0.01/GB-month**. Infrequent Access has higher operation prices, a **$0.01/GB retrieval fee**, and a **30-day minimum storage charge** even if deleted/moved earlier; lifecycle transition itself incurs a Class A operation. Egress is listed as free. [Cloudflare R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/), [Cloudflare object lifecycles](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/)

**Recommendation:** keep seven-day grace objects in Standard; evaluate 30-day transition to Infrequent Access only for accepted-product media genuinely expected to remain for 90 days with rare reads. For small/short-lived or frequently accessed objects, the minimum-duration, retrieval, request, and transition charges can outweigh the storage saving. First reduce bytes through camera-only capture, bounded image dimensions/quality, the existing 15-second video limit, duplicate suppression, and abandoned-upload cleanup. Measure actual GB-months and reads before selecting the storage class; do not prolong retention merely to make Infrequent Access economical.

## 5. Launch decisions for counsel and the owner

1. Approve the platform/seller/internal-runner/gig-runner contract map and customer-facing supplier/refund responsibility.
2. Reconcile partial-abort settlement rules with FCCPA sections 120, 122, 130, and 132, and ensure statutory remedies survive the 30-minute product dispute window.
3. Confirm the final status of FCCPC's 2026 Regulations and Guidance Notes from the Official Gazette; update record periods, distance-contract terms, and marketplace controls if final text differs.
4. Complete NDPC classification/registration, DPO workflow, DPIA filing, processor agreements, cross-border assessment, and breach procedures; document a lawful basis per onboarding field.
5. Confirm the NIMC/NINAuth licence and integration path with the identity provider; remove unnecessary raw identifier/document retention.
6. Approve any criminal-record, guarantor, biometric, or vehicle checks only for demonstrated roles/risks and provide correction/human review.
7. Approve a State-specific police-report and evidence-disclosure runbook that rejects coercive self-help.
8. Approve finite media/evidence periods, hold-release authority, R2 lifecycle/lock permissions, deletion verification, and the corresponding privacy-notice changes.

The safest overall posture is **minimum identity data, maximum transaction accountability**: verified participants and payouts, honest offers and receipts, photo-backed custody, prompt consumer redress, neutral incident review, lawful police escalation, and automatic deletion after a defensible finite period.
